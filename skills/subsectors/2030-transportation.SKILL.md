# Transportation subsector

GICS industry group 2030; parent [sector guide](../sectors/20-industrials.SKILL.md). Apply [the daily funnel](../../docs/v1/sector-workflow.md) and source comparison. Use actual current members; nonmember counterparties remain research entities.

Choose airlines, rail, trucking, logistics or transport infrastructure. For airlines, compare passenger traffic/load factors with yield, available capacity, unit costs excluding/including fuel and booking quality. For freight, inspect shipment/tonnage, rates, mix, network capacity, fuel surcharge and labor. Higher volume need not improve margin when pricing collapses.

Distinguish actual traffic from forecasts/revised estimates, seasonal from underlying changes, and fuel hedge/surcharge timing from spot prices. Read issuer operating reports and customer/shipping evidence; a macro traffic series is not its current result.

Query `"issuer" traffic yield capacity unit cost guidance` or `"issuer" freight volume rate surcharge`, then strike, accident, congestion and maintenance queries. Countercheck recession exposure, safety/regulatory restrictions, cancellations, excess capacity and capex. Compare same-mode/geography peers.

Watch named networks, fuel/labor costs and customers. A safety event needs official corroboration and severity/context, not a rumor-driven sell. Sources: [BTS traffic methodology](https://www.bts.gov/browse-statistical-products-and-data/statistical-products/air-traffic-methodology), [EIA fuel context](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/).

Store the chosen operating branch, KPI definitions/periods, peer comparisons, primary/counterevidence and exclusions in `SCREENING`; carry material drivers into the company profile/watchdog. Missing material facts mean blocked research, never improvised certainty.
