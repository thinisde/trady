# Energy sector

GICS sector 10. Apply [the daily funnel](../../docs/v1/sector-workflow.md), source independence and the common entry gate. This guide defines research questions, not an order rule.

Separate exploration/production, integrated firms, refining, pipelines/storage and equipment/services; the same commodity move can help one and hurt another. Use the [Energy group guide](../subsectors/1010-energy.SKILL.md) to choose a branch.

Compare crude/gas curves, inventory changes, product demand, refinery utilization, outages, hedges and capital discipline with current issuer exposure. Ask whether supply disruption or durable demand caused today's move, and whether producers actually receive the quoted benchmark price. A headline about a geopolitical event needs verified physical/economic transmission.

Check refining margins rather than assuming expensive crude improves refinery profit; midstream contracts and service backlogs can lag spot prices. Countercheck slowing demand, inventory revisions, unplanned downtime and debt/capex. Do not trade oil futures through an equity thesis.

Query separate primary inventory/production releases and issuer guidance, then independent reporting and counterevidence: `"issuer" production hedge realized price outage guidance`. Store product/region, observed period and uncertainty. Annual production context is not a current quote.

Sources: [EIA petroleum balances](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/), [EIA gas weekly](https://www.eia.gov/naturalgas/weekly/).

Store the observations, adverse evidence, selected branch/group, calculation references and blockers in the `SCREENING` artifact. A high sector priority never substitutes for company research or entry confidence.
