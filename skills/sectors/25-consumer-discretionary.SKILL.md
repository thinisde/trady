# Consumer Discretionary sector

GICS sector 25. Apply [the daily funnel](../../docs/v1/sector-workflow.md), source independence and the common entry gate. This guide defines research questions, not an order rule.

Compare [Autos](../subsectors/2510-automobiles-components.SKILL.md), [Durables & Apparel](../subsectors/2520-consumer-durables-apparel.SKILL.md), [Consumer Services](../subsectors/2530-consumer-services.SKILL.md) and [Distribution & Retail](../subsectors/2550-discretionary-distribution-retail.SKILL.md). A travel boom does not imply strong housing or vehicle affordability.

Examine real disposable income/spending, rates and credit, price versus volume, promotions, inventory and segment-level customer mix. Nominal sales growth can reflect inflation. Distinguish orders/bookings from fulfilled revenue and store growth from comparable-store demand.

Identify who bears tariffs, fuel, wage, financing and fulfillment costs, including contract/hedge lags. Countercheck cancellations, delinquency, discounting, return rates and a deteriorating low-income customer cohort. Compare like periods and calendar/holiday effects.

Query `"issuer" comparable sales traffic ticket inventory promotion guidance` plus a relevant financing/customer counterquery. Choose the group whose new facts support both demand and economics; a popular brand alone is not a catalyst.

Sources: [Census retail/housing indicators](https://www.census.gov/economic-indicators/), [BEA spending](https://www.bea.gov/data/consumer-spending).

Store the observations, adverse evidence, selected branch/group, calculation references and blockers in the `SCREENING` artifact. A high sector priority never substitutes for company research or entry confidence.
