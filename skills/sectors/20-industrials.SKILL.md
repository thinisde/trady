# Industrials sector

GICS sector 20. Apply [the daily funnel](../../docs/v1/sector-workflow.md), source independence and the common entry gate. This guide defines research questions, not an order rule.

Compare [Capital Goods](../subsectors/2010-capital-goods.SKILL.md), [Commercial & Professional Services](../subsectors/2020-commercial-professional-services.SKILL.md) and [Transportation](../subsectors/2030-transportation.SKILL.md). A procurement announcement, a service renewal and freight-volume change have different timing and margins.

Look for orders versus shipments, funded backlog, cancellations, production capacity, labor/input costs, working capital and aftermarket revenue. Compare organic growth with acquisition/FX effects; backlog growth can coexist with poor cash conversion or fixed-price losses. Rate and infrastructure themes require a real exposed issuer/segment.

Use production/durable-goods context and original contracts/results. For defense, distinguish a ceiling/options from funded awards; for transport, distinguish volume from yield and fuel surcharge. Countercheck supplier bottlenecks, strikes, contract losses, safety issues and weak end-market orders.

Query `"issuer" orders backlog conversion margin working capital guidance`, then relevant customer and regulator statements. Record whether the new information changes near-term earnings or only a multiyear total addressable market.

Sources: [Federal Reserve G.17](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/), [Census M3](https://www.census.gov/manufacturing/m3/).

Store the observations, adverse evidence, selected branch/group, calculation references and blockers in the `SCREENING` artifact. A high sector priority never substitutes for company research or entry confidence.
