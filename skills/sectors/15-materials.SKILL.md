# Materials sector

GICS sector 15. Apply [the daily funnel](../../docs/v1/sector-workflow.md), source independence and the common entry gate. This guide defines research questions, not an order rule.

Do not treat chemicals, metals/mining, construction inputs, paper and packaging as one commodity bet. Narrow through the [Materials group guide](../subsectors/1510-materials.SKILL.md) by actual product/end market and revenue exposure.

Compare output prices with feedstock/energy/freight costs, inventory and demand in construction, autos, electronics and agriculture. Look for volume versus price/mix, operating rates, capacity additions, mine disruptions and currency/geographic exposure. A metal supply constraint may help a producer while hurting a downstream fabricator.

Check whether a proposed project is financed, permitted and producing; a resource estimate is not profitable production. Countercheck substitution, weak orders, new supply, environmental liabilities and input inflation. Today's catalyst must be newer than the slow baseline statistics.

Query `"issuer" "product" volume price mix feedstock capacity guidance` and `"project" permit delay cost`, then compare two peers with the same product exposure. Store the margin transmission hypothesis and evidence against it.

Sources: [USGS commodity statistics](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center/commodity-statistics-and-information), [Census industrial indicators](https://www.census.gov/economic-indicators/).

Store the observations, adverse evidence, selected branch/group, calculation references and blockers in the `SCREENING` artifact. A high sector priority never substitutes for company research or entry confidence.
